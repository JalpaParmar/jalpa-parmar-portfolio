ReviewPulse

Mobile Customer Feedback & Product Insights

I looked at fictional mobile reviews to explore how feedback can inform product decisions without letting an average rating decide the roadmap.

Project type: Independent Portfolio Prototype
My role: Product Analysis / Product Management
Focus: Customer Feedback · Product Insights · Prioritization · Mobile Products · Metrics · Decision Support

SYNTHETIC DATA

ReviewPulse is an independent portfolio project using synthetic mobile-app review data. It does not contain customer information, employer data or proprietary application reviews.

02

Problem

An average star rating does not explain what people struggle with, whether an issue is increasing, or which journeys deserve investigation. I created ReviewPulse to show how I would organize written feedback into useful product signals.

03

How I Analyzed the Feedback

I grouped 180 original fictional reviews across iOS and Android, eight themes and four fictional versions, dated June–September 2026. Each review has a date, platform, version, rating, text, sentiment, primary theme and review type.

I assigned the labels for this exercise, then compared frequency, low ratings and patterns by version and platform. The dashboard computes its metrics from those records. No model, scraping or external service is involved.

One primary theme per review keeps counts from double counting. It also simplifies mixed feedback, so I would read the text before treating a category as the whole story. This constructed sample is not representative research.

04

What I Found

Check the checkout signal

14 of 17 payment reviews tagged 5.2 are negative in the full synthetic sample. Other versions: 2 of 11.

I would compare release changes, support incidents and checkout telemetry before concluding that the version caused a problem.

Look across both platforms

Negative login reviews: 6 iOS and 6 Android in the full synthetic sample.

I would trace recovery and session flows, then compare reproduction steps with authentication telemetry.

Separate preference from failure

8 notification feature requests and 8 negative notification reviews in the full synthetic sample.

I would investigate delivery failures separately and keep preference controls in discovery until the need is clearer.

Make finding things easier

4 usability comments and 8 negative search reviews in the full synthetic sample.

I would observe filter use and empty-result recovery, alongside search-to-detail and booking funnel data.

Correlation is not causation. Versions are associated with successive months in this sample. I cannot separate release effects from time or review composition. I would validate a pattern against support data, telemetry and release changes.

05

From Insight to Product Decision

I would investigate checkout reliability first in this exercise because it affects a core transaction journey and has repeated negative feedback. Authentication needs a cross-platform investigation. Notification controls belong in discovery, while search clarity needs observation of real tasks.

The prioritization matrix combines feedback frequency, customer impact, business relevance, confidence and estimated effort. All factors except frequency are explicit planning assumptions. The score structures a discussion; it does not approve a solution.

Checkout: a decision gate

I would check release changes, support incidents, checkout failure telemetry, platform differences and payment methods. Only if the problem is corroborated would I define focused reliability requirements. The accompanying brief is conditional, with proposed acceptance criteria rather than a delivery promise.

07

What I Would Measure / Validate

I would establish baselines for checkout completion and failure, authentication recovery, search-to-detail progression and reminder delivery. I would pair those with support themes, task observation and accessibility testing.

For feedback quality, I would check label consistency, sample coverage, changes in review mix and the proportion of mixed-topic comments. I would agree targets only after validating the problem. No business improvement is claimed here.

08

What I Learned

I found it useful to separate an observed signal from a proposed solution. Frequent feedback can justify investigation without being strong enough to decide what to build.

I would not make a roadmap decision from reviews alone. Support data, product analytics and release context would help establish whether a pattern reflects a wider problem.
