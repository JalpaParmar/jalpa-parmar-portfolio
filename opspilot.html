OpsPilot AI

AI-Powered Support & Engineering Operations

From unstructured customer issues to reviewed engineering work. An interactive product prototype with transparent suggestions, human approval and a shared operational picture.

Launch Demo ↗View Source
01

The problem

Support and engineering teams can lose time translating unstructured customer issues into actionable engineering context. Rewriting summaries, finding an owner and following up on status can fragment the handoff.

02

Product hypothesis & approach

AI-assisted interpretation can improve handoff consistency when suggestions stay transparent and human-reviewed. I designed this independent prototype around a decision checkpoint: reviewers can inspect the original request, correct the output and approve a named owner.

The target users are Support Leads, Technical Project Managers, Engineering Leads, QA Leads and Product Managers. The MVP focuses on one complete handoff rather than integrations: normalization, triage, review, routing, engineering work, status synchronization and operational digests.

03

Try a two-minute workflow

  1. Launch the demo and open SUP-101 from the overview.
  2. Read the original report, suggested tests and routing rationale.
  3. Edit priority or summary, choose an engineer and approve the handoff.
  4. Move the engineering work to In Progress, then Resolved. Support becomes Ready for customer confirmation.
  5. Open the evening digest and inspect AI Recommendation Quality to see your feedback reflected.
04

Architecture & technical choices

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules run entirely on GitHub Pages. Synthetic fixtures feed deterministic analysis rules; a shared in-memory state connects the queue, work board, digests and analytics. No backend, account, API key or live model is used.

05

AI suggestions, human decisions

Category-specific templates produce a summary, priority suggestion, potential component and validation scenarios. Illustrative confidence and a sample related-issue signal are explicitly labeled. Routing uses a visible rule score for queue, platform, component expertise and modeled workload; it is not machine learning.

Human approval creates one engineering work item. The reviewer controls summary, priority, queue and engineer. Engineering resolution does not close the customer conversation or approve a release.

06

Responsible AI & tradeoffs

Human review, editable output, visible confidence and correction metrics are implemented. Data is fictional and session-only. Refreshing resets the demo, making it easy to replay during an interview.

Production would need sensitive-data redaction, role-based access, durable audit records, secure adapters, calibrated evaluation and recovery from synchronization failures. These safeguards are design requirements, not implemented compliance claims.

07

What I would measure

Time to first human review, handoff completeness, incorrect-priority rate, reassignment rate, missed duplicates, blocked-work age and customer-confirmed resolution. Compare results with a reviewed baseline and inspect disagreements by category.

The demo measures reviewed suggestions, unchanged approvals, priority and routing corrections, edited summaries and requests returned for information. These are synthetic interaction metrics, not professional achievements or evidence of business impact.

08

Future possibilities

Evaluate the workflow with representative users, then add saved scenarios, robust integration adapters and a small labeled evaluation dataset. Knowledge retrieval, historical pattern analysis, release correlation, SLA-risk prediction and real LLM integration remain future concepts.
