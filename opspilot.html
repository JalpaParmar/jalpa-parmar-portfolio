I designed TriagePilot AI to demonstrate how I approach a cross-functional operational problem—from problem definition through workflow design, technical architecture, governance and prototype validation.

The implementation uses synthetic data and deterministic logic. It is not a production system and does not represent a former employer’s product or internal workflow.

01

The problem

Support and engineering teams lose context when customer issues move between queues. Manual summaries, unclear ownership and repeated status follow-ups make a simple handoff harder than it needs to be.

02

The solution

TriagePilot AI brings AI-style analysis, human review, routing recommendations and engineering work into one transparent flow. Status visibility, digests and analytics keep people informed after the handoff.

AI suggests

Summaries, categories, priority, validation scenarios and routing.

People decide

Review, edit, approve, assign, escalate and make release decisions.

The hypothesis: editable recommendations can improve handoff consistency. The prototype explores this with fictional data; it does not claim measured business results.

Requirements & scope

I modeled the MVP around preserving the original request, making ownership explicit, requiring human approval and keeping support status aligned with engineering.

In MVP

  • Support queue and structured triage
  • Summary, classification and priority recommendation
  • Validation scenarios and routing recommendation
  • Human approval and engineering work tracking
  • Status visibility and operational digest
  • Feedback metrics

Future / out of scope

  • Real external integrations and production LLM
  • Authentication / RBAC and production database
  • SLA prediction and production duplicate detection
  • Real customer communication
  • Automated release decisions remain out of scope

Architecture & responsible AI

Transparent by design

Deterministic templates and routing rules run in the browser. Suggestions are editable; approval gates engineering work. Confidence is illustrative, not calibrated.

Clear production boundaries

Real integrations would require access control, sensitive-data redaction, durable audit and evaluated model output. This demo makes no compliance claims.

Risks, governance & delivery dependencies
  • Incorrect priorityHuman approval before engineering handoff.
  • Sensitive data exposureOnly synthetic data here. Production would require minimization, redaction and secure server-side model access.
  • Incorrect routingVisible rationale and reviewer override.
  • Duplicate workA one-work-item-per-request guard rejects repeated approvals.
  • Over-reliance on AIIllustrative confidence, editable output and correction metrics; acceptance is not accuracy.

For production delivery, I would confirm support-field mappings, engineering ownership, QA acceptance criteria, adapter access and security review before rollout. These are planning dependencies, not existing integrations or compliance claims.

A production implementation could connect to an evaluated LLM through a secure server-side service. This portfolio prototype intentionally uses deterministic logic for repeatability, transparency and zero credential exposure.

What I would measure & learn

Portfolio demo metrics

Suggestions reviewed and accepted, human corrections, routing overrides and summaries edited. These describe synthetic session interactions.

Potential production measures

I would measure time to first review and engineering owner, reassignment rate, handoff completeness, blocked-work age and recommendation correction rate.

Learning / next iteration

Observe representative users completing synthetic handoffs. Review disagreements and decision clarity before considering evaluated AI or secure integrations.

Compare against a reviewed baseline. No improved business outcomes or professional impact metrics are claimed.

Portfolio implementation created independently using synthetic/sample data and original code. It demonstrates approaches informed by professional experience without reproducing proprietary employer systems, data or confidential workflows.
