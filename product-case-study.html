SmartServe

Product Management Case Study

An independent concept for a small service business to capture customer requests and coordinate appointment changes without losing the next action.

TypeIndependent product conceptRoleProduct Management / Product DefinitionEvidenceFictional personas and assumptions
01 / OVERVIEW & PROBLEM

Keep a request from getting lost

A customer asks to change an appointment. The coordinator needs to see the request, clarify availability, assign the next step and confirm the outcome. This concept starts with that narrow journey. It makes no claim that field research or a live launch has taken place.

02 / USERS

Two sides of the same handoff

Service coordinator

Needs one place to review incoming requests, ownership and pending responses.

Customer

Needs a simple way to request a change and know when it is confirmed.

These are fictional personas to test the product definition. I would validate the journey with real users before committing to a build.

03 / PRODUCT APPROACH & MVP

Make the next action clear

RequestReviewAssignConfirm

The MVP covers request capture, a coordinator queue, status and ownership, and a customer confirmation. I kept payments, automated scheduling and broad analytics out of the first scope so the core handoff can be tested.

Example user story: As a coordinator, I want to see unassigned appointment changes so I can respond and assign an owner. Acceptance: an incoming request appears with its time, status and contact context; assignment changes its owner and remains visible.

04 / PRIORITIZATION & ROADMAP

Choose with stated assumptions

I used illustrative RICE scores to compare features, then checked whether the highest score supports the narrow user journey. Scores are discussion inputs, not a substitute for feasibility or user evidence.

NOW

Core request flow

Capture, queue, assign and confirm.

NEXT

Templates and history

Reduce repeat coordination after the basics work.

LATER

Integration options

Explore calendar sync after validating demand and complexity.

06 / SUCCESS METRICS & LEARNING

Measure the handoff

I would measure request completion, time to first response, unassigned requests, customer confirmation and reasons for abandonment. I would start with a small pilot, review missed handoffs with users and adjust the MVP before expanding scope.

Independent product concept. Personas, prioritization inputs and roadmap are illustrative; no real user research, delivery result or employer material is claimed.
