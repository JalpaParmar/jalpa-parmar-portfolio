02 / THE PROBLEM

A green sprint board can hide a risky release

Completed work alone does not tell me whether regression is done, a dependency is late or a critical defect is unresolved. I need a short view that makes those tradeoffs visible before a milestone decision.

04 / METRICS THAT MATTER

Small set, clear meaning

Predictability

40 planned · 34 completed · 85% Say/Do. I use this as one signal. A lower result starts a discussion about scope changes, dependencies, estimates or capacity; it is not a score for judging people.

Flow

Throughput: 34 completed items. Cycle time: 5.2 days average. WIP: 9 items. Flow efficiency: 38% active work time compared with total elapsed time. I look for waiting and handoff friction, not just a sprint finish.

Quality

12 open defects, 3 critical, 84% regression complete, 1 sample escaped defect. I would review the trend and unresolved severity before recommending a release. These are example observations, not improvement claims.

I would not use velocity alone to judge delivery health. The figures need context from the team.

06 / AI-ASSISTED REPORTING

A draft for leadership, checked by the PM

Regression is progressing, but three critical issues and one integration dependency require attention before release approval. The committed scope is 85% complete, with two blocked items affecting the current milestone.

PM actions

  • Review critical issues with engineering and QA.
  • Escalate the integration dependency and confirm its owner.
  • Confirm QA capacity and regression plan.
  • Reassess readiness with stakeholders after evidence changes.

I would verify the underlying data before sharing an AI-assisted summary. The release decision belongs to people.
